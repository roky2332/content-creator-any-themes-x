## 1. The business starts before the first image

[ILLUSTRATION: 01-character.png]

Meet Mia

She's 24. She likes fitness, travel, fashion and coffee shops. She's friendly, confident, playful and relaxed

Instead of generating a different attractive woman every day, you build one recognizable character. Same look, same personality, same lifestyle

Why it matters: people don't follow a collection of random images. They follow an identity. Your character needs to feel like someone with a life

Before you generate anything, define three things:

- **Appearance:** face, hair, eyes, body type, recognizable features
- **Personality:** how she talks, what she likes, how she behaves
- **Lifestyle:** the places, activities, outfits and situations in her content

You're not creating an image. You're creating a brand

## 2. Create the character

The first technical step is her base appearance

Nano Banana Pro can create the initial images. Cream can refine the result

The goal is one strong reference image that keeps her identity the same in every future generation

**Prompt 1. Create the base character**

```text
Create an original fictional adult female AI influencer, 24 years old.

She has [describe hair], [describe eye color], [describe facial features], and a natural, attractive appearance.

Her style is modern, feminine, and realistic. Create a high-quality portrait with realistic skin texture, natural lighting, detailed facial features, and professional photography.

She must look like a unique fictional person, not a copy of any real individual.

Avoid distorted facial features, unnatural skin, excessive retouching, or unrealistic proportions.
```

Got a result you like? Save it. That's your master reference

**Prompt 2. Keep the same identity**

```text
Use the uploaded reference image as the identity reference.

Generate the same fictional adult woman. Preserve her facial structure, eyes, nose, lips, hairstyle, skin tone, and overall appearance.

Change only the following elements:
- Scene: [describe location]
- Outfit: [describe clothing]
- Pose: [describe pose]
- Expression: [describe expression]

Maintain the same recognizable identity and realistic photographic style.

Do not redesign her face or create a different person.
```

The most important rule: consistency. Never redesign her from scratch

## 3. One AI model, 30 days of content

[ILLUSTRATION: 02-content-library.png]

A creator business needs a steady flow of content

A simple starting system:

- 30-40 lifestyle and personality photos
- 10-15 short video concepts
- 10-20 extra images in different scenes and outfits
- 7 days of captions and story ideas

These are planning targets, not quotas. The goal is a content library before you start posting

Split it into four categories:

1. **Lifestyle:** coffee shops, travel, fitness, everyday activities
2. **Personality:** opinions, questions, humor, relatable moments
3. **Fashion:** outfits, locations, transformations
4. **Exclusive:** content for paying subscribers that follows platform rules

Claude turns this library into a publishing plan

**Prompt 3. A weekly content strategy**

```text
Act as a social media content strategist.

I'm building a fictional adult AI influencer named Mia, age 24.

Her personality is friendly, confident, playful, and relaxed. Her interests include fitness, travel, fashion, coffee shops, and everyday lifestyle.

Create a 7-day Instagram content plan.

For each day, include:
- One Reel idea
- One photo or carousel idea
- One Story idea
- A short caption
- A strong opening hook
- A simple call to action

Make the content feel natural, varied, and connected to the same character.

Avoid repetitive ideas and generic influencer captions.
```

One session. Several days of posts. No more creating everything from scratch every morning

## 4. The biggest upgrade: make her move

Photos are useful. Short videos get attention

Seedance 2.0 and Kling can turn images into video or recreate the movement of a reference clip

A photo of Mia in a coffee shop becomes a short clip. She turns her head, smiles, looks outside, adjusts her posture

**Prompt 4. Animate the character**

```text
Animate the woman from the uploaded image.

She is sitting in a modern coffee shop. She slowly turns her head, gives a natural smile, slightly changes her posture, and looks toward the window.

Use realistic human movement, subtle facial expressions, and natural camera motion.

Preserve her original facial identity, hairstyle, clothing, and environment.

Avoid facial distortion, unnatural movements, flickering, or changes in appearance.
```

Then edit in CapCut Pro. Subtitles, timing, format for social media

A useful starting target: 10-15 short videos a week. Test formats. Keep what brings profile visits

A video isn't enough on its own. It needs a reason to exist. A hook, a recognizable situation, an interesting moment

## 5. Make people discover her

No traffic, no subscribers

Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, Snapchat and Telegram can all help. Starting everywhere at once gets overwhelming fast

Start with one main platform and one extra traffic source. For example, Instagram as the main channel

An aggressive schedule:

- 3 Reels a day
- 2 carousels a week
- 2 Stories a day

This is a growth experiment, not a rule. If quality drops, cut the volume and fix the content

**Prompt 5. Reel ideas**

```text
Act as a short-form video copywriter.

Create 15 Instagram Reel ideas for a fictional adult AI influencer named Mia.

Her niche is lifestyle, fashion, fitness, travel, and everyday moments.

For every idea, provide:
- A strong opening hook
- A short video concept
- A suggested visual scene
- A natural caption
- A call to action

Keep the ideas short, engaging, and suitable for Instagram Reels.

Avoid clickbait, repetitive concepts, and generic motivational content.
```

Now, how the funnel works

Say your content gets 100,000 views in a month. Not everyone visits the profile. Not everyone who visits clicks. Not everyone who clicks subscribes

[ILLUSTRATION: 03-funnel.png]

One hypothetical example:

| Funnel stage | Example result |
|---|---:|
| Total content views | 100,000 |
| Profile visits (2%) | 2,000 |
| Link clicks (10%) | 200 |
| Paid subscriptions (10%) | 20 |

100,000 views. 20 new paying subscribers

These percentages are assumptions to show the funnel, not industry averages. Your results could be very different

The lesson: views alone don't tell you if the business works. Track what happens after someone sees your content

## 6. Turn followers into subscribers

Interest needs somewhere to turn into money

Platforms like Fanvue offer subscription pages and monetization tools. Before launching, check the current rules on AI-generated content, adult content, identity verification and synthetic media disclosure

Revenue sources:

- Monthly subscriptions
- Paid posts
- Content bundles
- Personalized offers
- Messaging and retention

A simple example. Subscription price: $9.99 a month

[ILLUSTRATION: 04-revenue.png]

| Paying subscribers | Monthly gross subscription revenue |
|---|---:|
| 25 | $249.75 |
| 50 | $499.50 |
| 100 | $999 |
| 250 | $2,497.50 |
| 500 | $4,995 |

That's gross revenue. Before platform fees, refunds, taxes and expenses. It also assumes every subscriber pays the full month

That's why retention matters

100 people subscribing once is one thing. 100 people paying month after month is a business

A welcome message introduces the page and sets expectations

**Prompt 6. Welcome message**

```text
Act as a community manager for a fictional adult AI creator named Mia.

Write a friendly welcome message for a new subscriber.

The message should:
- Thank them for subscribing
- Introduce Mia's page and the type of content they can expect
- Sound warm, natural, and personal
- Ask what type of content they enjoy most
- Avoid pressure, unrealistic promises, or manipulative language

Do not falsely claim that an automated message was personally written or sent by a human.
```

The goal: a welcoming experience and a reason to stay

## 7. A system that doesn't depend on you doing everything

At first, you'll handle everything yourself

As the library grows, manual work gets harder to manage

A simple workflow:

- **Google Drive:** images, videos, prompts, finished content
- **Notion:** publishing calendar and content status
- **Claude:** planning, captions, repetitive tasks
- **CapCut:** video editing
- **Scheduling tools:** organized publishing

You can also explore Claude with MCP integrations. MCP, the Model Context Protocol, lets compatible AI tools work with connected services

Instead of sorting every idea by hand, you build a workflow that classifies ideas, prepares captions and updates your content calendar

Start small. Don't automate everything before you understand the process

[ILLUSTRATION: 05-weekly-system.png]

A practical week:

| Day | Main task |
|---|---|
| Monday | Generate images and plan content |
| Tuesday | Create short videos |
| Wednesday | Edit and organize content |
| Thursday | Prepare captions and schedule posts |
| Friday | Review performance |
| Saturday | Test new content ideas |
| Sunday | Prepare next week's workflow |

Less repetitive work. Same control over quality

## 8. When to launch a second AI model

The common mistake: three or four characters before the first one has a working system

More characters means more images, more videos, more accounts, more conversations, more planning

Make the first one work. Track this every week:

| Metric | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Reel views | Whether content attracts attention |
| Profile visits | Whether viewers want to learn more |
| Link clicks | Whether your profile creates interest |
| New subscribers | Whether traffic converts |
| Renewals | Whether subscribers stay |
| Expenses | How much it costs to operate |

[ILLUSTRATION: 06-find-the-leak.png]

100,000 monthly views but only 20 link clicks? The problem is your profile, positioning or call to action

500 link clicks but only 5 subscribers? The problem is the landing page, offer, price or audience match

Find where people leave the funnel before spending more on traffic or launching another character

Add a second model only when the first has a repeatable workflow, a clear audience and measurable results

## 10. A 30-day plan for your first AI creator

You don't build the whole business in a day

[ILLUSTRATION: 08-30-day-plan.png]

**Week 1. Build the character**

- Define appearance and personality
- Generate a master reference image
- Create 15-20 consistent images
- Prepare the social media profile

**Week 2. Build the content library**

- Generate another 15-20 images
- Create 5-10 short videos
- Write captions and hooks
- Organize everything in Google Drive or Notion

**Week 3. Launch the traffic funnel**

- Start publishing consistently
- Test different Reel formats
- Add a clear profile link
- Prepare the subscription page
- Check the platform's content and disclosure requirements

**Week 4. Analyze and improve**

- Review views, profile visits and link clicks
- Find the strongest formats
- Check subscribers and retention
- Improve the profile and the offer
- Prepare next month's content

After 30 days, the goal isn't a specific amount of money

It's one complete system. A recognizable character, a content library, a traffic source, a monetization page and a way to measure results

## The biggest misconception about AI OFM

People think the money comes from generating an attractive AI girl

It doesn't

The image is only the surface. Behind it you need a consistent identity, a content strategy, distribution, a conversion funnel, retention and a workflow that doesn't collapse when you get busy

The numbers make it clear. 100,000 views mean little if nobody visits the profile. Hundreds of clicks mean little if the offer doesn't convert. New subscribers mean little if nobody stays

So what decides whether an AI creator makes money: the girl, or the system behind her?
