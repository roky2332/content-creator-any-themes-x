# Money Stories, стиль 3: эталоны (2026-11-26)

Правила: `guidelines/money-playbook.md`, раздел 2в. Источник: 4 поста другого аккаунта X, присланы автором вместе с видео. Видео расшифрованы целиком, кадры 0–5 с просмотрены. Факты в постах не образец: проверять.

## Общий разбор
- 380–430 слов. Хук 4–5 предложений, последнее предложение — исход
- «In the video below» 2-м абзацем: шоу + год + 3–5 дословных цитат (сверено с расшифровками: совпадают)
- Хроника с датами, свежий повод в конце («December 2026…»)
- «Do the math.» → «I think…» → «The case for / against» → строка с суммами → вопрос → подпись «I post one old money story every day, told simply.»
- **Видео не про деньги** (3 из 4): деньги из прессы, ролик даёт героя и эмоцию. Клипы 1:08–1:59, официальные каналы, вшитые субтитры. Начало часто контекст (кадр сериала, вопрос ведущего, архив)

## 1. Patricia Richardson: $25 млн против $50 млн
**Видео:** ET «Leading Ladies of the '90s», 1:17. 0:00–0:03 кадр из Home Improvement, с 0:03 она в студии. Цитаты: «there's this guy, he said, stand up» 0:02, «didn't want to do any more sitcoms… thankless mom» 0:13–0:17, «I did it for the money and to get out» 1:04, «pay you another pilot fee… let you out in May» 1:07. Про $25 млн в видео ничего нет

```text
Patricia Richardson says she took Home Improvement "for the money and to get out." Eight years later ABC offered her $25 million for one more season. Tim Allen was offered $50 million. She asked for the same money as him. ABC said no, and one of the biggest sitcoms on TV ended instead.

In the video below, from ET's Leading Ladies of the '90s, she explains how she got the part. Disney had her under contract. She "didn't want to do any more sitcoms" and "certainly didn't want to be a thankless mom." Her co-star was "this guy, a stand-up" she had never heard of. So the studio offered her another pilot fee and promised to let her out in May if the show wasn't picked up. "I did it for the money and to get out."

1991. Home Improvement starts on ABC and gets picked up. Richardson plays Jill Taylor, the wife of Allen's Tim, for eight seasons.

By the last season Allen is reportedly making $1.25 million an episode. Richardson says that over the years she was never paid even a third of what he made.

1999. Midway through season eight, ABC offers a 25-episode ninth season: $1 million an episode for her, $2 million for him.

She asks for the same rate as Allen. She has three children and has just been through a divorce. Later in the same interview she puts it simply: "there's not enough money in the world to do the ninth year."

ABC refuses. Home Improvement ends in May 1999 after eight seasons.

Do the math. $1 million times 25 episodes is $25 million. Allen's offer was $50 million. The gap between them was $25 million, the same as her whole offer.

I think she won the argument and lost the money, and she knew it going in. When you ask for a number you know they won't pay, you're not negotiating, you're leaving. She came in for the money and a way out. Eight years later the money wasn't enough to keep her.

The case for her: she was half the show and was offered half the money.
The case against: $25 million for one season was still one of the biggest offers any TV actress had ever had.

$25 million offered. $50 million to him. $0 taken.

Was she right to walk away, or should she have taken the $25 million?
```

## 2. Michael Richards: $600 000 за серию и ни цента с повторов
**Видео:** Today (NBC) 2024, 1:08. Richards в кадре с 0:00, «One on One». Цитаты: «anger had a hold of me, and I cancelled myself out» 0:20, «exodus» 0:38. Про деньги в видео ничего

```text
Michael Richards, Kramer on Seinfeld, asked NBC for $1 million an episode in 1997. He got $600,000, and to get it the cast reportedly gave up a share of the reruns. The next year Jerry Seinfeld and Larry David reportedly made $250 million each from syndication. Nine years later Richards' career was over.

In the video below, on Today in 2024, his first TV interview in years, Richards talks about the night that ended it. "Anger had a hold of me," he says. "I canceled myself out." He calls it an "exodus" from show business.

1989. Seinfeld starts. Richards, Jason Alexander and Julia Louis-Dreyfus are on small salaries.

1993. The three get a raise to about $150,000 an episode.

1997. They ask for $1 million each, what Jerry makes. Richards later told Howard Stern the show generated "about a billion dollars," so "why wouldn't the people who made it happen participate in the earnings of the show?" If NBC had said no, "we would not have come back." NBC settles at $600,000. Alexander later said the price was giving up a cut of the reruns.

1998. The show ends. Jerry turns down more than $100 million for a 10th season.

2000. The Michael Richards Show is cancelled after eight episodes.

2006. At the Laugh Factory, Richards yells racial slurs at hecklers. It is filmed. His career ends that night.

2024. He publishes a memoir, Entrances and Exits.

December 2026. Seinfeld and Larry David are booked for NBC's 100th anniversary special. Richards is not on the list.

$600,000 an episode is about $15 million for the last season. $250 million each for the two men who owned the show. The three actors who played Kramer, George and Elaine owned none of it.

I think the money split was decided in 1997, not in 2006. The rant ended his career. The contract had already decided he would never get rich from Kramer.

The case for the cast: they were half the reason people watched, and they signed away the part that paid forever.
The case for NBC: Jerry and Larry created the show, and $600,000 an episode was a record for supporting actors.

$1 million asked. $600,000 paid. $250 million each for the owners. Zero from the reruns.

Should Kramer have gotten a share of the reruns, or was $600,000 an episode already fair?
```

## 3. Ted Danson: закрыл Cheers на пике
**Видео:** MSNBC, Katie Couric на съёмочной площадке Cheers, 1993, 1:14. 0:00 ведущая в студии, 0:01 вопрос Couric, Danson с 0:05. Цитаты: «on my hands and knees begging… one more year» 0:13–0:18, «me who's pulling the plug» 0:35, «false ego» 1:01, «time to move on» 1:12. Про деньги в видео ничего

```text
Ted Danson, the star of Cheers, was reportedly paid $450,000 to $500,000 a week when he decided to end the show in 1993. NBC and Paramount had expected a 12th season. The finale drew 80 million viewers. Four months later his co-star Kelsey Grammer was back on the same stage in Frasier, and he went on to become the highest-paid actor in TV history.

In the video below, Danson talks to Katie Couric on the Cheers set a week before the finale. A year earlier, he says, "I was on my hands and knees, begging these guys to come back. 'Please, one more year.'" This time it was him: "All of a sudden, it was me who's pulling the plug." Leaving while you're on top, he says, is "false ego." "It just seems like it's time to move on."

1982. Cheers starts on NBC. It nearly gets cancelled after its first season.

1992. Danson talks the cast into one last year.

April 1993. Asked why he is quitting, he jokes: "Money. I was making too much money."

May 1993. The finale airs. It is the most-watched episode of any show in the 1990s.

September 1993. Frasier premieres on the rebuilt Cheers stage.

2001. Grammer signs for two more seasons of Frasier at more than $1.6 million an episode, a TV record.

December 2026. Danson and Grammer are both booked for NBC's 100th anniversary special.

Do the math. Even a short season of 22 episodes at $450,000 is about $10 million Danson said no to. Grammer stayed, and in Frasier's last two seasons he made more than $75 million.

I think Danson was right for himself. He has said his life was "a hot mess" back then. But Cheers was not only his show, and the rest of the cast found out the money was over when he did.

The case for Danson: 11 years, 273 episodes, and nobody owes a network one more season.
The case against: a year earlier he begged everyone to stay, and then he was the one who left.

$500,000 a week. 80 million viewers. $1.6 million an episode for the man who stayed on.

Did Danson walk away at the right time, or did he end the show for everyone else?
```

## 4. Prince Naseem Hamed: 25% тренеру
**Видео:** ITV News, 1998, 1:59. 0:00–0:06 архив (лимузин) с голосом за кадром, затем репортаж. Цитаты: «he upset me and my family» 1:04, «He's got the problem» 1:35 (по смыслу говорит Ingle), «made a man a millionaire» 1:40, «twisted and bitter» 1:56. Единственный из четырёх, где в видео есть деньги

```text
Prince Naseem Hamed, the Sheffield boxer who became a world featherweight champion, was trained from the age of 7 by Brendan Ingle, who reportedly took 25% of his purses. When they split in 1998, Ingle said money had turned the champion's head. Hamed's answer: he had "made a man a millionaire." James Bond actor Pierce Brosnan now plays Ingle in the film Giant.

In the video below, archive footage from 1998, Hamed says Ingle's book "upset me and my family." Ingle, in the studio, shrugs: "He's got the problem." Hamed replies: "I've been with a man 17 years and made a man a millionaire." Then he calls Ingle "twisted and bitter."

Age 7. Hamed walks into Ingle's gym in Wincobank, Sheffield.

Age 11. Hamed says this is when Ingle made a deal with him: £10 million once he had earned £40 million. That is the 25%.

October 1998. Hamed beats Wayne McCullough in Atlantic City with Ingle in his corner.

1998. Ingle's book says money has turned Hamed's head. In December the split is confirmed after 17 years, and Hamed blames the book.

April 1999. Ingle tells a reporter Hamed will be finished as a top fighter within two years.

2018. Ingle dies at 77.

January 2026. Giant opens in UK cinemas, largely from Ingle's side. Hamed says he regrets how it ended.

Do the math. 25% of £40 million is £10 million. Hamed says the deal was struck when he was 11. By the time it fell apart, Ingle had trained him for 17 years.

I think a 25% cut feels fair when a boy has nothing and enormous when he is world champion. Both men can be telling the truth about the same deal.

The case for Hamed: he took the punches, and a deal with an 11-year-old is hard to call a negotiation.
The case for Ingle: he built the champion, and his great-nephew wrote that Brendan did worry Hamed's brother Riath was turning Naseem's head over the cut.

25% of the purses. £10 million on £40 million. 17 years together. One deal at 11.

Was 25% a fair price for making a champion, or a deal no 11-year-old could really agree to?
```
