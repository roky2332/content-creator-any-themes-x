## 1. Hugh Hefner: allowance в Playboy Mansion
**Разбор:** Сцена-ритуал без says; деньги ($1,000 + $2,000); вопрос режиссёра «prostitution?» и ответ; разница в возрасте; итог: смерть 2017 + вдова со своей версией; вопрос-добивка с цифрой.

```text
Every Friday morning, the women living at the Playboy Mansion walked into Hugh Hefner’s bedroom, sat on his bed and asked for their money.

“Hi honey, I’m here for my allowance.” It was $1,000 a week, plus $2,000 for outfits for the parties at the mansion.

In the video below, his former girlfriends describe the routine to A&E’s Secrets of Playboy. They liked to go in pairs.

Then the director asks the question everyone was thinking. “What about the people who say it’s prostitution?”

“It’s not prostitution. I was working for a company.”

She describes it as a deal. They followed his rules. He paid the allowance, covered their insurance and bought them cars.

“He needed us to be at his side and look pretty. So it was a trade. It was understood.”

Hefner was in his 70s. Most of the women in his bedroom were in their early 20s.

“We just knew that we lived in his world and we had to follow the rules.”

Hefner died in 2017 at 91. Now his widow, Crystal Hefner, is telling her own version of what happened behind that door.

$1,000 a week for a place in the most famous house in America. Was it a job, or exactly what the critics said it was?
```

## 2. Sarah Edmondson / NXIVM: клеймо
**Разбор:** Обман-разоблачение («was told… It was a brand»); масштаб $100 млн = $18,000 в день; «Blackmail was the actual term»; приговоры трём фигурантам с цифрами.

```text
Sarah Edmondson was told she was getting a tattoo. It was a brand, the initials of a man an heiress had paid more than $100 million. They burned it into her skin with a cauterizing pen.

The man was Keith Raniere, founder of a self-help group called NXIVM. The heiress was Clare Bronfman of the Seagram liquor fortune. Prosecutors say she spent more than $100 million on Raniere and his group, about $18,000 a day for 15 years.

Inside the group he ran a secret circle of women who called each other masters and slaves. To get in, each woman handed over collateral, including nude photos of herself. In the video below, Edmondson explains what it was for. “Blackmail was the actual term,” she says. It was held over her head “to do whatever was being asked of me.”

Smallville actress Allison Mack recruited women into the circle, one by one. Edmondson says Raniere wanted three things: “endless money, sex and power.” She only understood the brand after she got out. “That’s something that pimps do to the women that they own.”

She went to the FBI in 2017. Raniere got 120 years for sex trafficking and racketeering. Bronfman got nearly seven years, a $500,000 fine and had to forfeit $6 million, a small part of a fortune put at $200 million. Mack, who brought him the women, served three years.

Should the women who recruited for Raniere be called victims, or did they get off easy?
```

## 3. Andrew Tate: вебкам

```text
Andrew Tate says it started with a $100,000 debt to “very dangerous people” and seven days to pay. He took $20,000 to a casino and lost all of it. Then he sat down and wrote a list of what he had left, including five girlfriends in five different cities.

He flew the women in and told them they were going to be rich. Two stayed, and that was the start of his webcam business. In the video below, he describes the system he built: it “allowed me to retain 100% control of their income.” And: “I know how to make sure the girl can’t run away.”

“I had these guys selling their houses, life savings, loans, all of it to me,” he says. Asked if he ever felt bad: “F*ck no.”

Some of the women tell it differently. Four have sued him in London, two of whom worked for the webcam business. They accuse him of physical and sexual violence between 2013 and 2015, and the trial is expected in 2027. UK prosecutors have authorised 21 charges against the brothers, including rape, human trafficking and controlling prostitution for gain. Both deny everything and still face a separate case in Romania.

Were the women in Andrew Tate’s webcam studio his business partners, as he says, or the product he was selling?
```

## 4. Joe Francis vs Steve Wynn
**Разбор:** Выручка в хуке; угроза из видео против показаний Quincy Jones; $40 млн → $19 млн = 630,000 DVD по $29.95; второй фронт (несовершеннолетние); ответ одним словом «No».

```text
Joe Francis says that if he had taken no for an answer, he wouldn’t be where he was. Girls Gone Wild made $20 million in its first two years and sold 4.5 million DVDs in 2001 alone. Then he left Steve Wynn’s casino owing about $2 million.

In the video below, Francis tells ABC News what he says Wynn wrote about him in an email to Quincy Jones. “He was going to hit me in the back of the head with a shovel and bury me in the desert.”

Jones took the stand and told a different story. He testified that no threat was ever made. Outside the courtroom, Wynn called Francis “a liar” and “a desperate, out of control, vicious guy.”

A Los Angeles jury gave Wynn $40 million. A judge cut it to $19 million, still about 630,000 Girls Gone Wild DVDs at $29.95 each.

The casino was not his only problem. A federal judge in Florida jailed him for contempt after he refused to settle with seven women who said they were underage when his crews filmed them. A man in the documentary says he first realized the girls were minors when he got arrested.

Asked on camera whether he feels bad for the girls, Francis answers with one word. “No.” Did the courts finally catch up with a man who got rich filming drunk teenagers, or did they punish him for being the most hated guy in the room?
```

## 5. Mr. Marcus: сифилис остановил индустрию
**Разбор:** Цифра карьеры (1,800) + удар ($14 млрд индустрия); его версия против версии прокуроров; дата 7 августа; приговор и иск $129,360.

```text
Mr. Marcus made more than 1,800 adult films. In 2012, a $14 billion industry stopped filming because of his syphilis test. He says a clinic worker offered to make the result disappear: “He said no problem, sat at the computer.” He got 30 days in jail.

In July 2012, his own doctor told him it looked like syphilis, and a blood test came back positive. Prosecutors said Marcus covered the positive result himself, photocopied the form and went back to work with women who had no idea.

In the video below, Marcus tells his version. He says the clinician promised the new results would “test for everything, but it won’t print” the syphilis. Asked whether he photocopied the test, he says: “I was trying to get back to work.”

On August 7, a producer noticed the altered form. Production across Los Angeles shut down while health officials looked into five possible cases, and investigators concluded he was the first performer infected. “You’re the cause of the outbreak,” he says people told him.

He pleaded no contest and got 30 days and 36 months of probation. In 2014, a court awarded one of the women, Lylith LaVey, $129,360, more than four times what he says he had to give up to keep working: nothing.

Did one man lie to keep his paycheck, or did an industry that couldn’t afford to stop make him its scapegoat?
```

## 6. Lenny Dykstra: детективы против судей
**Разбор:** says + $500,000; «What was the spread last night?»; «Fear does a lot to a man»; опровержение судьи; банкротство и камера рядом с врачом Michael Jackson.

```text
Lenny Dykstra says he paid $500,000 to private investigators to dig up dirt on baseball umpires. Some gambled, some cheated on their wives, and he made sure they knew he knew. A few years later, the World Series champion was bankrupt and sleeping in a cell next to Michael Jackson’s doctor.

In the video below, Dykstra explains the plan. “I just pulled half a million bucks out, hired a private investigation team.” When an umpire called a strike, he says he would turn around and ask, “What was the spread last night?”

In 1993 he led the National League in walks. “You think I led the league in walks? I don’t think, I know.” Asked whether he bribed them, he answers: “Fear does a lot to a man.”

Former umpire Larry Barnett called the story “the stupidest thing I’ve ever heard.”

In 2009, Dykstra filed for bankruptcy, listing less than $50,000 in assets and between $10 million and $50 million in debts. He later went to prison for bankruptcy fraud and grand theft auto. He says his cell was six by eight feet, with Conrad Murray on one side and the head of the Mexican Mafia on the other.

Was Dykstra a genius who found an edge nobody else would use, or a broke man inventing a legend?
```

## 7. Darryl Strawberry: секс между иннингами и помилование
**Разбор:** Три удара в хуке (секс, контракт, помилование Трампа); «Some of them covered for me»; налоги, кокаин, рак; «Send me to prison»; вопрос про помилование.

```text
Darryl Strawberry says that during Mets games, he would slip back to the clubhouse between innings to have sex, and some teammates covered for him. Then he signed a five-year, $22.25 million deal with the Dodgers and started using crack in Hollywood. Thirty years after his tax evasion conviction, Donald Trump pardoned him.

In the video below, he tells Dr. Oz how it worked. “I would go between innings and stuff like that and run back and have a little party going on. And I thought it was pretty cool.” Asked whether the coaches knew: “Some of them covered for me.”

He won the World Series once with the Mets and three times with the Yankees. In Los Angeles, he says, the parties found him: “I got introduced to it in LA, you know, Hollywood.”

In 1995 he pleaded guilty to tax evasion. He served six months of home confinement and repaid $350,000 in back taxes and penalties. After that came a cocaine arrest, two rounds of colon cancer and years of probation.

In the end, he asked a judge to lock him up. “I said I’ll take the 12 months. Send me to prison.”

When the pardon came, Strawberry wrote that it had “nothing to do with politics.”

Does a pardon wipe out what a man admits he did, or only what a court said he did?
```

## 8. Jose Canseco и Madonna

```text
Jose Canseco says Madonna's people called to ask if he was single because she wanted a baby with a man born in Cuba. At the time he was the highest paid player in baseball. Years later, he says, he was living in a garage with $20 in his pocket.

In the video below, Canseco describes their first meeting at her house. She took him into her bedroom, the blackout blinds came down, and she played him a scene from her own movie, Truth or Dare. "So what do you think about that?" she asked. He told her he had a game to play.

In 1988 he became the first player ever with 40 home runs and 40 stolen bases. In 1990 Oakland gave him five years and $23.5 million, the richest deal in the game. He says he made about $55 million in his career. Forty percent went to taxes. He told Inside Edition his divorces cost him $7 million to $8 million. He says he lost another $11 million when the stock market crashed. In 2008 the bank took his house in Encino.

That same year he told Us Weekly that Madonna wanted to marry him, and when he said leaving his wife would cost him money, she told him she had plenty. Now he says, "Madonna was a five. And her money didn't impress me."

Did Canseco really walk away from Madonna, or is a broke man still selling the best story he has?
```

## 9. Antonio Cromartie: 14 детей

## 10. Darryl Strawberry: внебрачный сын

```text
Darryl Strawberry says a woman used to show up at the ballpark in a No. 18 Strawberry shirt, trying to flag him down. He was the biggest star on the Mets, married, with two kids. One night in St. Louis, he says, "it just happened." Months later she was pregnant, and she sued him.
```

## 12. Curt Schilling: 38 Studios
**Разбор:** says + $50 млн + «I'm tapped out»; кровавый носок как символ; $75 млн гарантия штата; дата банкротства; ирония: носок продан за $92,613; деньги штата против его доли.

```text
Curt Schilling says he put more than $50 million of his own baseball money into a video game company. When a radio host asked him if he was broke, he said: "I'm tapped out, if that helps."

In the video below, Schilling says he watched teammates on the road "jeopardize their marriages" while he stayed in and played video games. With $35 million of his own money in the company, he says it made him "$35 million more nervous." Years later he asks: "What do people want me to apologize for?"

Schilling won three World Series. In 2004 he pitched on a stitched up ankle, and the bloody sock made him a Boston legend.

After he retired, he started a game studio called 38 Studios. In 2010 Rhode Island gave him a $75 million loan guarantee to move the company there from Massachusetts. He says he never took a salary.

His first game came out in February 2012. On June 7 that year, 38 Studios filed for bankruptcy and every employee lost their job. Schilling says he sat his family down and told them the money he saved in baseball was probably gone.

In 2013 he sold the bloody sock at auction for $92,613. Rhode Island taxpayers were left holding the bonds. The state later won back about $61 million in settlements, and Schilling's share was $2.5 million, paid by the company's insurer.

He says there was no malice and no criminal intent. "People are angry. And I'm angry."

Was Schilling a dreamer who lost everything on a bad bet, or did he gamble with a whole state's money?
```
