# Референсы ниши Hollywood Backstories (присланы автором, 2026-11-12)

Пять постов автора, на которых построена ниша. Факты в референсах не образец: в новых постах всё проверять заново.

## 1. Dana Carvey и Dr. Evil
```text
Dana Carvey says Mike Myers, his partner in Wayne's World, stole Dr. Evil from him. According to Carvey, the character grew out of his impression of their boss at SNL, Lorne Michaels.

Myers turned it into the villain of Austin Powers, and the three films made $676 million. New Line reportedly paid Myers $25 million for the third film alone. Carvey got nothing.

In the video below, Carvey remembers cheering for his friend at the premiere, and then Dr. Evil came on screen. "That's the song I wrote, that's the music I heard in my head."

Carvey only did his Michaels impression backstage. Doing it in public, he says, meant "biting Lorne Michaels, the hand that fed me."

He says he even invented Dr. Evil's famous pinky at the corner of the mouth. "The pinky thing was a made-up thing. Lorne doesn't do that."

Myers tells it differently. To him, Dr. Evil is mostly Blofeld, the Bond villain, and only "a little bit Lorne Michaels." And the pinky, he says, was Lorne's own: "Lorne has a pinky thing, but he doesn't do it anymore."

Carvey never talked it through with Myers and went to a therapist instead. Twenty-two years after the premiere, Howard Stern asked if he had ever called Myers about it. "I haven't done that."

They grew close after playing Wayne and Garth again in a 2021 Super Bowl ad. A year later, Carvey said they had become "very, very close friends."

Did Myers steal from his friend, or just put on screen what Carvey never dared to do in public?
```

## 2. Ryan Reynolds и утечка Deadpool
```text
Fox wouldn't move forward with Ryan Reynolds' Deadpool until the test footage leaked in 2014. The film went on to gross over $782 million worldwide on a $58 million production budget.

In the clip below, Jimmy Fallon asks Reynolds about the leak. "I'm 70% sure it wasn't me."

Reynolds says he spent 11 years trying to get the film made. After Green Lantern flopped in 2011, he nearly gave up on Deadpool. Fox approved a test clip from director Tim Miller but still wouldn't make the movie. Reynolds compared those years of starts and stops to "the worst relationship I'd ever been in."

The clip leaked online in July 2014, and fans wanted to see the movie. By September, Fox had announced a release date.

To stay within budget, the writers had to rework the script. Screenwriter Paul Wernick recalled: "In 24 hours we cut $7 million from the script." Reynolds says the studio wouldn't let the writers work on set. He paid them out of his own salary so they could revise scenes and dialogue during filming.

Deadpool opened in February 2016. It took in over $13 at the worldwide box office for every dollar of its production budget.

In 2025, Reynolds acknowledged his role in the leak at the Toronto International Film Festival: "Yes, I cheated a little."

Would Deadpool ever have made it to theaters if the test footage had stayed at Fox and fans had never seen it?
```

## 3. Molly Bloom и Dan Bilzerian
```text
Hollywood's rich lost millions at poker in Molly Bloom's Four Seasons suite, up to $7,000 a night. She threw out Dan Bilzerian for winning, because he was "gonna bankrupt" them.

She lived off their tips, and in 2009 her income topped $4 million. The ones who played worst tipped the most.

In the video below, Bilzerian explains why the invitations stopped. "She stopped inviting me back because she realized I was, you know, gonna bankrupt those f*cking guys."

Inside there were women in cocktail dresses, every kind of alcohol, sushi and "a full f*cking buffet." "You tipped her like three to five percent if you wanted to get a seat back," he says.

Bilzerian remembers: "She had dirt on most of these motherf*ckers, banging chicks while they had wives." So "if they didn't wanna pay, she had that f*cking ace in the hole."

The "suckers didn't really have to" tip, "but they tipped probably more than anybody." Bilzerian himself "made a little bit of money" at that table, and then the invitations stopped.

In 2013, Bloom was arrested with 33 others in a $100 million gambling and money-laundering case. She pleaded guilty, got probation and forfeited $125,000, before Jessica Chastain played her in Molly's Game.

Did Bloom throw him out to protect her players, or her tips?
```

## 4. Bradley Cooper и сосед Will Arnett
```text
Bradley Cooper once bashed his head on a concrete floor at a party to look tough, then did it again. He spent the night in a hospital waiting for stitches, and at 29 he was addicted to cocaine.

In 2011, People named him the Sexiest Man Alive, and by 2014 Forbes had him earning $46 million a year. He credits his neighbor Will Arnett, who told him, "You were a real asshole, man."

In the video below, Cooper says what he would have lost if he hadn't stopped. "I would never be sitting here with you."

It started with a $70,000 loan for acting school and a part on the TV show Alias. By season two he was so sidelined he thought, "I want to f*cking kill myself."

Addicted to cocaine, he left the show and tried to seem funny by copying mean comedians like Arnett. At another dinner with Arnett in July 2004, he remembers "thinking I was so funny."

Arnett, who lived next door, came over and told him, "You were a real asshole, man." Then he saw the dogs at the door, and at four in the afternoon Cooper still hadn't walked them.

"That was like the first time I ever realized I had a problem with drugs and alcohol," he says. He quit at 29 because it hit him: "I'm actually gonna ruin my life."

Five years later, The Hangover made him a star, and by 2012 he asked $15 million for the third one. He has 12 Oscar nominations, and in 2020 Brad Pitt said of him, "I got sober because of this guy."

Did Cooper save himself, or did it take one brutal sentence from a neighbor?
```

## 5. Glenn Close и «культ»
```text
From age 7 to 22, Glenn Close lived inside Moral Re-Armament, a movement she now calls "basically a cult." When she left, the only thing she knew how to do was act.

Acting brought her eight Oscar nominations without a single win. On November 15, the Academy finally gives her an honorary Oscar.

In the video below, Close explains on the radio what those years did to her. "It was incredibly destructive for all of us."

Her parents joined when she was 7, and the family moved between the movement's centers. For two years they lived at its headquarters in Caux, Switzerland.

The movement preached "four absolutes": honesty, purity, unselfishness and love. "Everybody spouted the same things," Close says, "and there's a lot of rules, a lot of control."

Members "weren't allowed to do anything," she says, or were "made to feel guilty about any unnatural desire." As a teenager she was "repressed, clueless and guilt-ridden."

To stay in, she had to tell someone "all the bad things" she had done. She calls it "a deep humiliation, but it's also a diminishing."

In the 1960s she toured with the movement's singing groups and married a guitarist from one of them. Looking back, she calls it close to "an arranged marriage."

At 22 she "just left and went to college." For years she wouldn't trust her instincts, because they "had all been dictated" to her.

The movement, now called Initiatives of Change, describes its mission as "building trust across the world's divides." Close says she has had no contact with it since she left.

Years after leaving, she saw a childhood trauma specialist. She has forgiven her parents: "They had their reasons for doing what they did."

Should parents be free to raise their kids inside a movement they believe in, or should children choose for themselves?
```
